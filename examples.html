Fourier Beyond Drawing

Most real signals are not periodic. Each example below makes a signal periodic first. Then it uses Fourier analysis. The method that makes the signal periodic controls the error.

1JPEG image compression

A block of pixels is not periodic. If you repeat the block, its two ends do not match, and you get a jump. If you mirror the block, the ends match. A mirrored signal needs only cosine terms. This is the DCT (discrete cosine transform).

JPEG applies the DCT to each 8×8 block in two directions. Then it removes the small high-frequency coefficients. Move the slider to keep fewer coefficients per block.

2MP3 and AAC audio compression

Music is not periodic. The encoder cuts it into short frames. Simple blocks have hard edges, so removed coefficients cause jumps at each block edge. The MDCT uses frames that overlap by half. A sine window makes each frame go smoothly to 0. When the frames add back together, the edge errors cancel.

A real encoder also uses a hearing model. It removes the frequencies that a person cannot hear, not only the high frequencies.

3Spectrum analysis and leakage

A tuner looks at a short window of sound. If the tone does not complete a whole number of cycles in the window, the window ends with a jump. The jump spreads energy into many frequencies. This is leakage. A Hann window makes the ends go to 0 and reduces leakage.

Set the slider to a whole number, for example 10. Then the rectangular window has no leakage, because the tone is periodic in the window. The STFT moves this window along the signal.

4The heat equation

A metal rod has length 1. Its two ends stay at 0 degrees. The start temperature is not periodic. Fourier made it periodic with an odd extension: he mirrored it upside down to the left side. An odd function needs only sine terms. Each term decays alone, as e−k²π²t. High frequencies decay fastest, so the temperature becomes smooth.

5Spectral methods

Weather and fluid models must calculate derivatives. In frequency space, a derivative is easy: multiply term k by ik. For a smooth periodic function, this spectral derivative is much more accurate than a finite difference with the same number of points. Here f(x) = esin x.

The longitude direction on Earth is periodic, so models use Fourier series there. For other directions, they use Chebyshev polynomials. A Chebyshev series is a cosine series after the change x = cos θ.

6Time series: find the season

Ten years of monthly data have a trend, a yearly season, and noise. The last value is much higher than the first. This jump puts a lot of energy into the lowest frequencies, and the season is hard to see. Remove the trend (a straight line) first. Then the season shows as one clear peak.

7From series to transform

A pulse of width 1 repeats with period T. Its Fourier series has terms at frequencies k / T. When T increases, the terms come closer together. When T goes to infinity, the pulse does not repeat, and the terms become a continuous curve. This curve is the Fourier transform.

Period T
T · ck at frequency k / TTransform: sin(πω) / (πω)

The series uses a sum over k. The transform uses an integral over ω. MRI scanners measure the Fourier transform of the body directly, then they invert it to make an image.
